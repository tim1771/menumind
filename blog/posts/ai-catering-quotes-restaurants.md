# The Catering Quote That Used to Take 2 Hours Now Takes 6 Minutes

**Meta Description:** Independent restaurants are losing catering orders because quotes take days. AI-assisted pricing and proposal generation closes the gap in minutes.

**Target Keywords:** AI catering quotes, restaurant catering pricing AI, catering proposal automation

---

A corporate office manager emails your restaurant on a Tuesday morning. She needs lunch for 45 people on Thursday — sandwiches, salads, two hot options, dietary accommodations for three vegans and one celiac, delivery to a downtown office at 11:45 AM sharp. Budget around $20 per head. Can you send a quote by end of day?

Your kitchen manager is in the middle of prep. You're meeting a supplier. The bookkeeper isn't in today. By the time someone sits down to actually price this thing, it's 4 PM. You start with the food cost, then try to remember whether the delivery fee covers the driver or just gas, then dig up the last catering invoice to see what you charged for paper goods, then realize you forgot to add the gluten-free upcharge, then second-guess the whole price because you don't know if she's getting two other quotes.

By the time you send a polished PDF at 5:30 PM Wednesday — almost 30 hours after she asked — she's already booked with someone else. You just lost a $1,000 order because your quoting process is built for 2015.

This is the single most common AI-fixable problem we see in independent restaurants that do catering. The food is great. The pricing is fair. The proposals are slow, inconsistent, and full of math errors. AI doesn't fix the food. It fixes the quote.

## The Real Cost of Slow Quotes

Catering inquiries are time-sensitive in a way most restaurant owners underestimate. A corporate buyer comparing three caterers will book the first one with a complete, professional proposal — not necessarily the cheapest. In our experience working with restaurants on their catering operations, the close rate on quotes sent within two hours is roughly two to three times higher than quotes sent the next day. After 48 hours, the deal is usually gone.

The math is brutal. If you receive 10 catering inquiries a week and your average ticket is $850, every quote you don't send is roughly $200 to $400 in lost expected value once you factor in close rates. Slow quoting at most independent restaurants is quietly costing $40,000 to $80,000 a year. Owners don't see it because the loss is invisible — a phone call that never came back.

The other hidden cost is inconsistency. When three different staff members can quote catering, you end up with three different prices for similar orders. Sometimes you underprice and lose margin. Sometimes you overprice and lose the order. Without a system, your catering pricing is essentially random.

## What AI Actually Does With a Catering Inquiry

The workflow that's emerging at restaurants doing this well looks like this.

An inquiry comes in by email, web form, or phone. The relevant details — headcount, date, dietary needs, delivery requirements, budget hint — get fed into an AI tool that's been trained on your menu, your food cost percentages, and your pricing rules. Within a few minutes, you have a complete draft proposal: itemized menu suggestions that fit the budget, calculated food cost, suggested upsells, delivery and labour fees, a polite cover note, and a clean PDF.

You read it, adjust anything that needs adjusting, and send. Total time from inquiry to sent proposal: 6 to 10 minutes. The customer gets a real quote before lunch.

The AI handles the parts that used to take an hour: pricing math, formatting, dietary substitutions, calculating per-head costs, applying your standard fee structure. You handle the parts that matter: confirming the menu makes sense for the occasion, deciding whether to discount for a valued repeat client, double-checking the delivery time is realistic.

## The Pricing Discipline AI Forces

Most independent restaurants have catering pricing that lives partly in a spreadsheet, partly in someone's head, and partly in "what we charged that one law firm in October." AI implementation forces you to write down your actual pricing logic, and that alone is worth doing.

When you set up an AI quoting tool, you have to define your target food cost percentage for catering — usually different from dine-in, often around 28 to 32 percent. You define your labour fee structure for events that need staff. You define your delivery zones and fees. You define your minimum order size and your rush surcharges. You decide whether paper goods are included or extra. You set your dietary substitution upcharges.

Half the operators we work with realize during this setup that they've been losing money on certain catering categories for years. The exercise of codifying pricing surfaces every pricing leak you didn't know you had.

Once the rules are in, every quote is mathematically correct and consistent. The $1,200 corporate lunch you quoted in March uses the same logic as the $1,200 corporate lunch you quote in November. Your gross margin on catering stops being a mystery.

## What the Customer Experience Looks Like

The shift on the customer side is dramatic, and underrated as a competitive advantage.

A corporate office manager who's used to waiting two days for a restaurant quote, then getting a one-paragraph email with a number at the bottom, opens your proposal and sees an itemized menu organized by dietary category, a clean cost breakdown, a delivery schedule, photos of the most popular dishes, your cancellation policy, and a clear next step to confirm. It arrives the same morning she asked.

You haven't just won the quote — you've signalled that you take catering seriously. Corporate buyers are not buying lunch. They're buying confidence that nothing will go wrong at their Thursday meeting. A polished, fast proposal is the first proof point that you're a reliable vendor.

In our experience, restaurants that move to AI-assisted catering quoting see close rates climb noticeably within the first quarter — often from somewhere around 20 to 25 percent to closer to 35 or 40 percent on similar inquiry volume. The food didn't change. The response did.

## The Canadian Tax Wrinkle

One detail worth getting right from the start: catering pricing in Canada has tax complexity that AI handles well and humans handle poorly. HST or GST/PST treatment differs by province, by item category, and sometimes by whether food is delivered hot, cold, or in a tray of more than six servings. An AI quoting tool configured for your province will apply the right tax structure automatically. A human assembling quotes at 4 PM on a Tuesday will sometimes get this wrong, and getting it wrong on a corporate invoice is the kind of thing that ends the relationship.

If you're in Ontario, Quebec, BC, or any province with overlapping sales tax categories, this is a quiet but real reason to systematize quoting.

## Where to Start Without Overcomplicating It

You don't need to rebuild your entire catering operation to start seeing results. The minimum viable version is straightforward.

Pick your three or four most common catering scenarios — corporate lunch boxes, hot buffet for 25 to 50, drop-off platters, full-service event for 75 to 100. Document the actual pricing logic for each. Feed that to your AI tool of choice along with your menu and dietary modifiers. Build templates for the cover note in your restaurant's voice.

Run the next month of inquiries through the new system in parallel with whatever you're doing now. Compare quote turnaround times. Track close rates. You'll have your answer within four to six weeks.

The restaurants that are quietly dominating local catering in 2026 aren't the ones with the best food. They're the ones whose quote hits the inbox before the office manager has finished her coffee.
